### 1.1 ROOT ROT is the hole (arm 2)

**+26.6 points from removing one Driver**, same seeds, same trio, same decks: 56.7% → **83.3%**,
seventeen fights flipped to the player and one against, **p = 0.00014**. That is the largest
single-lever effect measured anywhere in tickets 71–76, and it lands the boss fight almost exactly on
the ~84.3% a passing gauntlet wants.

For contrast, Tidewrack's TIDAL SURGE was measured **inert** in the same kind of isolation
(p = 1.000, one discordant pair in thirty). The two Drivers are not in the same weight class and
nobody had checked.

### 1.2 The comp candidate is a null (arm 4)

`ratatoskr_v1 → ratatoskr_v2` moves the boss fight **56.7% → 55.0%**, eleven discordant each way,
**p = 1.00**. It is a clean null rather than a small effect, and it is worth having: the session now
opens knowing the composition is *not* where Rootfall's difficulty lives, which is the opposite of
what ticket 74 found at Tidewrack. Henry may still want a different candidate; this one is answered.

### 1.3 Where runs die (arm 1)

Already measured, not re-run: **73.3 / 66.7 / 56.7**. The boss is the worst cell but the lead-ins are
also 11–18 points under the ~84.3% guide, so Rootfall is not a boss problem the way Tidewrack was —
**it is soft everywhere, and worst at the boss.** ROOT ROT off fixes the boss and leaves a compound
of roughly 0.733 × 0.667 × 0.833 ≈ **40.7%**, which is still 19 points short. **One lever does not
close this gym.**

### 1.4 The party-lean bracket (arm 3)

Water-lean beats the type-correct fire-lean party by **10 points of compound** (37.8% vs 27.7%) —
while bringing *the wrong element* to a Nature gym. The gain is entirely in the rolled fights
(81.7/81.7 against 73.3/66.7); at the boss the two are **identical at 56.7%**.

That is the ticket-73 launch-triangle question showing up in the gauntlet, and it now has a shape:
**the type advantage does what it says at the authored boss, and raw deck strength dominates the
rolled fights, where the enemy pool is element-mixed and the advantage averages out.** Nature-lean is
worst at the boss (38.3%) exactly as the triangle predicts.

`p = 0.098` at n=60 — suggestive, not established. It wants n=120 before it is ruled on.

## 2. Ticket 75 rulings 1a/1b — the toolbox diagnosed

### 2.1 No card is a bad printing (ruling 1b)

Rootfall's three answers, one at a time into the bare deck:

| arm | compound | paired vs bare |
|---|---|---|
| bare | **27.7%** | — |
| + `scrubber` only | 27.1% | 31 vs 31 · **p = 1.00** |
| + `vent` only | 29.3% | 30 vs 33 · p = 0.80 |
| + `drip_feed` only | 28.2% | 31 vs 32 · **p = 1.00** |
| + two (`selective`) | 20.5% | 38 vs 27 · p = 0.21 |
| + all three | **11.1%** | 49 vs 18 · **p = 0.00019** |

**Every card is individually free. The basket is not.** One card costs nothing measurable, two cost
7 points of compound, three cost 17 — and only the full basket reaches significance.

**This is the answer ruling 1b was for: there is nothing here to reprice.** A card whose own arm is
p = 1.00 has no number to move. Whatever is happening is a property of *holding several* at once.

### 2.2 …but Emberfall's cards DO cost, individually (ruling 1b, second gym)

The same treatment at Emberfall gives the opposite shape, which is why it was worth running:

| arm | fight 1 | fight 2 | boss | compound | paired vs bare |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bare | 90.0% | 80.0% | 86.7% | **62.4%** | — |
| + `hamstring` only | 85.0% | 85.0% | 73.3% | 53.0% | 26 vs 18 · p = 0.29 |
| + `discharge` only | 85.0% | 78.3% | 75.0% | 49.9% | 26 vs 15 · p = 0.12 |
| + both (`selective`) | 83.3% | 73.3% | 71.7% | **43.8%** | 31 vs 14 · **p = 0.016** |
| + all three | 88.3% | 76.7% | 65.0% | **44.0%** | 30 vs 14 · **p = 0.023** |

### 2.3 The two gyms do not share a curve, and that IS the finding

| cards held | Rootfall | Emberfall |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 27.7% | 62.4% |
| 1 | 27.1 / 29.3 / 28.2 — **free** | 53.0 / 49.9 — **−9 to −12pt each** |
| 2 | 20.5% (−7.2) | 43.8% (−18.6) |
| 3 | 11.1% (−16.6) | 44.0% (−18.4) |

**Rootfall is superlinear: no card costs anything alone, the basket costs 17 points.**
**Emberfall is additive-then-saturating: each card costs ~9–12 alone, two cost 19, the third is free.**

Neither of the two available stories covers both. "Bad printings" fails at Rootfall, where every
card's own arm is p ≈ 1.00 and there is no number to reprice. "Buys too many" fails at Emberfall,
where two cards already cost the full loss and a single card costs half of it. Energy cost fails
too: Emberfall's damage is carried by its two **1-energy** cards, and dropping its only 2-energy
daemon changed nothing.

**No mechanism is asserted.** Ruling 1 said not to before these arms; having run seven of them I
still cannot name one that fits both gyms, and the honest report is that rather than the
best-sounding of three that each fit half the data. Candidates that remain live and are NOT
distinguished by anything measured here: draw contention (a situational card is a dead draw in a
5-turn fight, and Emberfall's fights are its shortest at 4.1–5.5 turns), AI misplay of held tech, and
an interaction between the answers and the specific boss each is aimed at.

**One concrete asymmetry worth a designer's eye rather than another arm:** at Emberfall the toolbox
damage concentrates on the **boss** fight — the one the cards were designed for — 86.7% bare to
65.0–75.0% with them. The cards aimed at a fight are costing the most *in* that fight.

## 3. What I would put in front of Henry

1. **ROOT ROT's number is the Rootfall lever**, and it is worth +26.6pt on its own. But 1.3's
   arithmetic says even a free boss fight leaves the gym at ~40.7% — **the lead-ins need something
   too**, and no ticket owns them at any gym.
2. **The comp is not the lever at Rootfall** (p = 1.00). Tidewrack's answer does not transfer.
3. **The toolbox behaves differently at the two gyms measured** (§2.3): Rootfall's cards are free
   alone and expensive together; Emberfall's cost ~9-12 points each. So there is **something to
   reprice at Emberfall and nothing to reprice at Rootfall**, and a single ruling covering both would
   be wrong at one of them. This is the point at which I would ask for a session rather than another
   arm — I have run seven and run out of hypotheses the data supports.
4. **Water-lean beating type-correct fire-lean by 10 points** is ticket 73's triangle in the
   gauntlet. Suggestive at p = 0.098; wants n=120 before ruling.

## 4. Reproducing

```
npx vite-node src/debug/balance/runRunGate.ts --bands gauntlet --gym gym_rootfall \
  --matchup favourable --iterations 60 [--toolbox selective|card:<id>] [--lean <Element>]
npx vite-node src/debug/balance/runRunGate.ts --bands gauntlet --cells gauntlet:fight2 \
  --gym gym_rootfall --matchup favourable --iterations 60 [--boss-relics off] [--tweak rootfall-rat-v2]
```

Raw reports in `76-runs/`. `--toolbox selective` reads `GYM_SELECTIVE_ANSWERS`; `--lean` reuses the
harness's own lineup picker with a different element so the bracket measures the lean rather than
anyone's team-building; `rootfall-rat-v2` is a knob and **the trio in the tree is unchanged**.
